# DRAFT Client Payment and Late Payment Policy

Status: Draft for solicitor review.

This policy is intended for business clients.

## 1. Payment in advance

Recurring services are paid before the service period begins.

One-off services are paid in full before work begins unless the Order Form states otherwise.

## 2. Payment methods

Payments may be collected through:

- Stripe
- CRM payment link
- bank transfer
- other approved method

## 3. Grace period

Kydos's current operating approach provides approximately 14 days to resolve a missed payment before service consequences, subject to the signed agreement.

A grace period does not waive the debt.

## 4. Reminder process

Suggested:

Day 0:
payment due/failure notification.

Day 3 to 5:
manual reminder.

Day 7:
second reminder.

Around Day 14:
service-pause notice where still unpaid.

The Agency may adjust timing reasonably based on circumstances.

## 5. Suspension

After appropriate notice, the Agency may pause work for unpaid invoices.

Paused services may affect:

- posting schedule
- ad management
- website work
- SEO work
- CRM support
- reporting

## 6. Late commercial payment rights

For qualifying business-to-business debts, UK law may allow statutory interest and fixed debt-recovery compensation.

Current GOV.UK guidance states statutory interest can be 8% plus the Bank of England base rate where a different contractual interest rate has not been agreed.

Fixed recovery amounts may also apply depending on the debt value.

The Agency reserves applicable statutory rights rather than automatically charging them on every invoice.

Official guidance:
https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery

## 7. Disputes

If the Client disputes an invoice, it should explain the reason promptly.

The undisputed portion should still be paid where appropriate.

## 8. Chargebacks

A chargeback does not automatically cancel the contractual debt.

The Agency may suspend services while a disputed/reversed payment is investigated.

## 9. Debt recovery

Persistent unpaid debt may be referred to:

- debt-recovery provider
- solicitor
- court process

after appropriate notice.

## 10. Consumer clients

If a client is not acting in the course of business, obtain legal review before applying business late-payment provisions.
