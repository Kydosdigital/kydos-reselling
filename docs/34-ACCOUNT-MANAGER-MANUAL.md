# Account Manager Manual

The Account Manager owns the client relationship and the strategic/content side of the account.

The Account Manager is not simply a scheduler.

## Core responsibility

The Account Manager is responsible for making sure the client receives relevant, accurate, on-brand work and understands what is happening on the account.

## Main duties

- client communication
- business research
- competitor research
- audience research
- monthly content strategy
- monthly content calendar
- captions and copy
- hooks
- CTAs
- hashtags
- community management where included
- reactive content decisions
- story publishing
- approval chasing
- client corrections
- monthly reporting
- briefing the Creative
- recording client rules
- escalating risks

## Capacity

Normal:
4 clients.

Upper working capacity:
5 clients.

Do not keep adding clients if deadlines, quality or communication begin to slip.

## Daily routine

- check client messages
- check approvals
- check scheduled content
- check comments/DMs where community management is included
- check pending creative
- update task list
- record new client corrections
- follow up on missing assets/access
- monitor urgent/reactive opportunities

## Weekly routine

- prepare weekly Creative briefs
- make sure the Creative has everything required
- review submitted designs/videos
- send approved-quality work to client
- chase approvals
- schedule/publish approved content
- update client status
- check next week's content readiness

## Monthly routine

Before the 25th:
- research upcoming month
- review previous performance
- review client priorities
- review important dates
- create full calendar
- run internal quality check

By the 25th:
Calendar should be ready internally.

Then:
- Operations Manager/CEO reviews
- Account Manager corrects
- calendar goes to client
- client review target is 3 days
- up to 2 revision rounds
- approved calendar moves into production

Month end:
- prepare monthly report
- record recommendations
- identify upsell/cross-sell opportunities
- identify churn risks
- refresh Client Content Rules Sheet

## Research standard

Before writing:

1. understand what the client sells
2. understand who buys it
3. understand what the audience cares about
4. inspect competitors
5. review recent client content
6. review performance
7. review client corrections
8. identify current business priorities
9. check claims that require evidence
10. check important dates/events

Do not write generic content that could belong to any business.

## Copy standard

Every post should answer:

- why should the audience care?
- what is the point?
- why now?
- what should they do next?

Avoid:
- empty motivational filler
- repetitive hooks
- long introductions that delay the point
- unsupported claims
- generic AI-sounding language
- jargon the client's audience would not use

## Client communication

Be:
- clear
- polite
- proactive
- concise
- accountable

If something is late:
say so early.

If something is unclear:
ask before producing the wrong work.

If a client gives a correction:
update the Client Content Rules Sheet.

## Community management

Only perform the level included in the client's package.

Never:
- argue publicly
- disclose confidential information
- improvise legal/medical/financial advice
- make refund promises
- admit liability
- delete serious complaints without escalation

Escalate sensitive issues.

## Reactive content

You may replace a planned item with relevant reactive/trending content where it clearly benefits the client.

Do not:
- chase every trend
- force irrelevant memes
- increase monthly volume without approval
- compromise the client's tone

## Quality check before sending creative

Check:
- correct client
- correct concept
- correct spelling
- correct offer
- correct CTA
- correct branding
- correct dimensions
- no factual errors
- no prohibited wording
- client corrections respected

## Escalate to Operations Manager/CEO when

- client is seriously unhappy
- client requests out-of-scope work
- client threatens cancellation
- payment is overdue beyond normal follow-up
- there is a compliance concern
- there is a public complaint with reputational risk
- there is a serious team-performance problem
