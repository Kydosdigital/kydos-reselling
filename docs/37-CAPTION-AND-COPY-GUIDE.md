# Caption and Copy Guide

## Purpose

Good copy should make the audience:

- stop
- understand
- care
- trust
- act

## Basic structure

Not every caption must follow the same formula, but a useful structure is:

1. Hook
2. Context/problem
3. Insight/value
4. Proof or explanation
5. CTA

## Hooks

Weak:
"Did you know social media is important?"

Stronger:
"If your business posts five times a week but nobody knows what to do next, frequency is not the problem."

## Write for the client's audience

Avoid language the business owner likes but the customer would never use.

Ask:
How would the customer describe the problem?

## Make one post do one job

Examples:
- educate
- build trust
- answer an objection
- show proof
- humanise
- promote
- drive enquiry
- explain process

Do not cram five unrelated messages into one caption.

## CTA

CTA should match the post.

Examples:

For education:
Save this for later.

For consideration:
Want us to look at your current setup? Send us a message.

For conversion:
Book a discovery call.

## Platform adaptation

### Instagram/Facebook

Can be conversational.

### LinkedIn

More context and professional insight.

Avoid turning every LinkedIn post into corporate jargon.

### TikTok/Reels

The visual hook matters more.

Caption should support, not repeat the entire video.

### X

Be concise.

## Claims

Do not invent:
- results
- statistics
- testimonials
- client outcomes
- qualifications

For health, legal, finance or regulated sectors:
check evidence and compliance.

## AI-assisted writing

AI can help with:
- brainstorming
- structure
- alternative hooks
- proofreading
- summarising research

The Account Manager remains responsible for:
- research
- accuracy
- tone
- originality
- final wording

Never send unreviewed generic AI copy to a client.
