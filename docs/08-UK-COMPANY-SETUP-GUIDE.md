# UK Company Setup Guide

Last reviewed: 31 August 2026

This guide is written for someone starting a UK digital marketing agency using the Kydos model.

It is general business education, not legal, tax or immigration advice.

## 1. Decide the business structure

The programme default is a private company limited by shares.

Why Kydos teaches this route:
- the company is a separate legal entity
- it is suitable for a recurring-revenue agency
- it can employ staff, use contractors and enter contracts
- it creates a clearer structure for banking, accounting, payroll and future growth

Some people may already have a company. They do not need to register another one simply to follow the programme.

## 2. Choose the company name

Before registering:
- check Companies House for existing or very similar names
- check the UK trade mark register
- make sure the matching domain is available
- avoid names that imply a government connection or use restricted words without permission
- for a private limited company, the legal name will usually end in Limited or Ltd

Kydos recommendation:
Choose a name that can still make sense if the agency expands beyond social media.

## 3. Decide the registered office

A UK limited company needs an appropriate registered office address in the same UK jurisdiction in which it is incorporated.

Important:
- the registered office is public
- do not use a home address if you do not want it displayed publicly
- an accountant, solicitor or registered office provider may be used with permission
- the company must also provide a registered email address, but Companies House does not publish that email address

## 4. Directors, shareholders and PSCs

A private company needs at least one director.

A director:
- must normally be at least 16
- does not have to live in the UK
- is legally responsible for running the company and ensuring statutory responsibilities are met

A company limited by shares needs at least one shareholder.

Identify People with Significant Control, normally including anyone who owns more than 25% of shares or voting rights or otherwise exercises significant control.

## 5. Identity verification

Companies House identity verification is now part of the company setup environment.

From 18 November 2025, new directors and relevant PSC appointments are subject to identity-verification requirements.

People who verify receive a Companies House personal code.

Do not leave this until the end. Make identity verification part of the company setup checklist.

## 6. Choose the SIC code

Kydos Digital uses:

73110, Advertising agencies

This is a sensible starting SIC code for a digital marketing agency.

A new agency should still select the code or codes that accurately describe what it actually does.

Do not add unnecessary SIC codes merely because the business might offer those services one day.

## 7. Choose the formation route

### Kydos recommended beginner route

Kydos used ANNA Money because it can combine company formation with business-account onboarding and administrative tools.

Use the current Kydos referral link or code when provided.

### Direct Companies House route

A company can also be incorporated directly.

As of 31 August 2026:
- digital incorporation fee: £100
- paper incorporation fee: £124

Fees can change, so always check the current Companies House fee page before publication or purchase.

## 8. Information to prepare before registration

Prepare:
- chosen company name
- registered office
- registered email
- director details
- shareholder details
- PSC details
- share structure
- SIC code
- service addresses
- identity verification details where required

## 9. After incorporation

Save these immediately:
- certificate of incorporation
- company number
- incorporation date
- articles of association
- memorandum
- statement of capital
- Companies House authentication code when received
- Companies House personal code for each verified person
- registered office details
- shareholder records

Create a secure folder:

Company Admin / Companies House

## 10. Immediate post-formation tasks

Within the first setup phase:
1. open the business bank account
2. connect accounting software
3. appoint or engage an accountant
4. buy the domain
5. create business email
6. set up Google Drive
7. set up CRM
8. create privacy and client documents
9. assess ICO data-protection fee obligations
10. set up payroll only when required
11. assess VAT only when relevant
12. create a compliance calendar

## 11. Non-UK founders

A director does not have to live in the UK, but the company still needs an appropriate UK registered office.

Banking, tax residency, immigration status and the right to work in the UK are separate questions.

Do not tell a participant that forming a UK company automatically gives them a right to work, live in the UK or sponsor themselves.

Where immigration is relevant, refer them to the programme's separate business-readiness section and the regulated immigration adviser.
