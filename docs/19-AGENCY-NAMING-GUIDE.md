# Agency Naming Guide

This guide helps a new agency owner choose a name that can grow with the business.

## What a good agency name should do

A good name should be:

- easy to pronounce
- easy to spell
- easy to remember
- suitable for a professional UK business
- broad enough to support future services
- available as a domain
- not confusingly similar to an existing company or trade mark
- usable across social media platforms

Avoid names that are too narrow if the agency may later expand.

For example, a name built entirely around "Instagram Posting" may become restrictive if the agency later sells SEO, paid ads, websites and CRM services.

## Kydos naming principle

Kydos Digital is broad enough to support:

- social media management
- paid advertising
- SEO
- website development
- CRM and lead automation
- email and SMS automation
- digital strategy

Programme participants should aim for the same flexibility.

## Step 1, Brainstorm

Create 20 to 30 possible names.

Possible naming styles:

### Founder-led

Examples:
- Smith Digital
- Taylor Media
- Ade Agency

### Abstract brand

Examples:
- Kydos Digital
- Brightline
- Northstar Media

### Outcome-led

Examples:
- Growth Foundry
- LeadCraft Digital
- Visibility Lab

### Location-led

Examples:
- Manchester Growth Studio
- Northern Digital Co

Location-led names can work well, but think about whether the business may later operate nationally or internationally.

## Step 2, Check Companies House

Search the Companies House register.

Check:
- exact name
- very similar names
- names that could create confusion

Do not assume availability on Companies House means the brand is legally safe.

## Step 3, Check trade marks

Search the UK Intellectual Property Office trade mark database.

Look for:
- exact name
- similar spelling
- similar sounding names
- related marketing, advertising, technology or business-service categories

Where the name is commercially important or uncertain, obtain professional trade mark advice.

## Step 4, Check domain

Prefer:
- .co.uk for a UK-focused agency
- .com where available
- ideally secure both if the budget allows

Avoid:
- unusual spellings
- excessive hyphens
- numbers unless they are genuinely part of the brand
- domains that people will struggle to type correctly after hearing the name once

## Step 5, Check social handles

Check:
- Instagram
- Facebook
- LinkedIn
- TikTok
- X
- YouTube where relevant

The handle does not need to be identical everywhere, but it should remain recognisable.

## Step 6, Final test

Ask:

1. Can someone spell it after hearing it?
2. Can it work on an invoice?
3. Can it work on a proposal?
4. Can it support SEO, ads, web and CRM later?
5. Does it sound credible when a salesperson says it on the phone?
6. Is the domain available?
7. Is the Companies House position clear?
8. Is the trade mark position acceptable?
