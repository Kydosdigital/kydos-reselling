# Client Escalation SOP

Not every client issue needs the owner.

Escalation should be proportional to risk.

## Level 1, Account Manager resolves

Examples:

- small copy correction
- routine approval delay
- simple scheduling question
- normal revision
- asset request
- platform question within scope

Account Manager:
- responds
- records
- resolves
- updates Client Content Rules Sheet where relevant

## Level 2, Operations Manager

Examples:

- repeated quality complaint
- recurring missed deadline
- conflicting client instructions
- repeated scope pressure
- serious dissatisfaction
- difficult approval bottleneck
- team performance affecting client
- repeated service issue

Operations Manager:
- reviews facts
- speaks with Account Manager
- agrees corrective action
- communicates clearly
- records outcome

## Level 3, Owner/CEO

Examples:

- cancellation threat with material commercial impact
- legal threat
- major refund dispute
- public reputational issue
- serious compliance risk
- data/privacy incident
- major financial error
- serious staff misconduct
- high-value scope dispute

## First response rule

Do not become defensive.

A useful structure:

1. acknowledge
2. clarify facts
3. explain what will happen next
4. give a realistic timeline
5. follow through

## Evidence

Before escalating, gather:

- agreement
- scope
- payment status
- approval history
- revision history
- WhatsApp/email messages
- task history
- files delivered
- internal notes

Do not escalate based only on one person's memory.

## Never admit liability casually

Account Managers and Operations Managers should not make legal admissions, refund promises or compensation commitments without authority.

## Post-incident

After resolution ask:

- what failed?
- was SOP followed?
- was SOP missing?
- was ownership unclear?
- should Client Content Rules Sheet change?
- should staff training change?
