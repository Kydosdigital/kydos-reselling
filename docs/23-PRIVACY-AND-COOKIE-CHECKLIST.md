# Privacy and Cookie Checklist

This is a practical website checklist, not legal advice.

## Privacy notice

The website privacy notice should explain:

- who the business is
- contact details
- what personal data is collected
- why it is collected
- lawful basis where relevant
- who it is shared with
- international transfers where relevant
- how long it is retained
- individual rights
- how to complain
- ICO details where appropriate

## Forms

For every form:

- [ ] collect only what is needed
- [ ] link privacy notice
- [ ] do not use pre-ticked marketing consent boxes
- [ ] keep service enquiry and marketing consent distinct where needed
- [ ] tell the user what happens after submission

## Cookies

Audit the website for:

### Strictly necessary

Examples:
- security
- login/session
- basket
- load balancing

### Analytics

Examples:
- Google Analytics

### Advertising

Examples:
- Meta Pixel
- Google Ads remarketing
- LinkedIn Insight Tag
- TikTok Pixel

### Functional

Examples:
- embedded tools
- chat widgets
- preference storage

## Consent

Where non-essential cookies require consent:

- do not fire them before valid consent
- offer meaningful accept/reject controls
- record preferences where required
- make it possible to change preferences

Do not use a banner that effectively forces "Accept" while hiding "Reject".

## CRM and tracking

Document:
- CRM provider
- analytics provider
- advertising platforms
- scheduling tools
- form tools
- hosting
- payment processor

## Website footer

Consider including:
- Privacy
- Cookies
- Terms
- Contact
- Company legal name
- company number
- registered office where appropriate

## Review

Recheck privacy/cookies when:
- adding a new ad pixel
- adding analytics
- adding live chat
- changing CRM
- changing payment processor
- adding embedded video/form services
- launching a new marketing automation

## Kydos programme rule

Every new agency website must pass this checklist before launch.

Where the business is regulated, processes sensitive data or has complex tracking, obtain specialist legal/data-protection advice.
